# Transferring your financial products and services to another financial institution

Learn more about transferring your financial products and services to another financial institution. Follow steps to transfer accounts, credit cards, loans and lines of credit, mortgages and registered products.

## Planning a product or service transfer
Moving products or services to another financial institution involves many steps and may take time. You may need to open the new product first and cancel the old one later.
Leave enough time to:

- transfer money
- receive any new cards
- update payments

### Tip
Plan before you start the transfer.
Keep track of your accounts and services during the process. This will help you avoid overdraft fees, missed payments and non-sufficient funds (NSF) fees.

Before transferring a financial product or service to another financial institution, ask whether any fees apply.
These may include:

- account transfer fees
- administrative fees related to moving products or services
Understanding these costs before you begin the transfer process can help you make an informed decision.

### Know why you may want to transfer a product or service
There are many reasons why you may want to transfer your products or services to another financial institution.
Examples include:

- to save on fees or interest charges
- to earn more interest
- to get products or services that better fit your needs
- you’re unhappy with the service at your current institution
- your financial institution is closing the branch where you hold your products and services
- another financial institution acquired yours
You may have to transfer your products and services if your financial institution fails. This is very rare in Canada, but it’s possible.

### Know what to do if your financial institution fails
If your financial institution fails, deposit insurance protects your eligible deposits.
The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protects eligible deposits at each of its member institutions. CDIC coverage is free and automatic. You don’t need to sign up.
If a CDIC member institution fails, CDIC will contact you. CDIC will reimburse insured deposits based on the records of the failed member institution.
Learn more about the Canadian Deposit Insurance Corporation.
A provincial or territorial deposit insurance provider may protect deposits at other financial institutions.
If your province or territory regulates your financial institution, you can contact the deposit insurance provider. They may tell you what happens if the institution fails.
Find your provincial or territorial regulator.
Learn more about deposit insurance in Canada.

### Review what you have now
Before you transfer your products or services to a new financial institution, there are things to consider. Make a list of all your products and services with your current financial institution, including automated transactions.
You may need to review:

- your online banking app or website
- a list of your products and services (provided by your financial institution)
- your monthly statements
- older documents from your financial institution
- your credit reports from Equifax and TransUnion
This may also help you find:

- what you need to transfer, close, cancel or stop
- which products and services you use or don’t use
- which products and services you want
- if you have inactive accounts
Learn more about getting your credit report and credit score.

### Identify your banking needs
Financial institutions offer many types of products and services.
Before you sign up for new products or services:

- think about how you’ll use them
- check the fees, if any
- evaluate the benefits, including lower fees and convenience, if any
- decide if you want all your products and services at the same financial institution

### Tip
Take time to compare what financial institutions offer to find what best suits your needs.
Make sure you understand the terms and conditions of the products the financial institution offers.

Learn how to choose financial products and services that are right for you.

### Check how your financial institution is regulated
Your rights and consumer protections depend on who regulates your financial institution.
For example, the federal government regulates some institutions. Provinces and territories regulate others.
Find out if your financial institution is federally regulated.

## Transferring your chequing and savings accounts
You may transfer deposit accounts like your chequing and savings accounts.
Before you close your current accounts, make sure you have:

- a list of your automated transactions
- your new account details
- enough time to transfer your direct deposits and pre-authorized debits
- enough money in your accounts to cover payments during the switch

### Identify your automated transactions
To find your automated transactions, you may:

- check your transactions over the last few months or the past year to find pre-authorized debits
- identify all direct deposits, including income from work, pension and benefits
- review your bills and budget so you don’t miss any payments

### Open a new account
If you meet certain conditions, banks, federal credit unions and authorized foreign banks must open a deposit account for you.
Review the terms and conditions and ask questions. Keep a copy of your account agreements.
Learn more about opening a bank account.

### Transfer your money
You may transfer your money from your current accounts to your new accounts.
Most financial institutions may help with transfers through:

- wire transfers
- Interac e-Transfers®
- linked accounts
- electronic funds transfers
- bank drafts
Ask your financial institution which option is best for moving money into your new chequing and savings accounts.

### Update your direct deposits and pre-authorized debits
Give your creditors and service providers your new banking information.

### Tip
Before payments start coming out of your new account, make sure there’s enough money in it to cover them. This may help you avoid missed payments and fees.

Ask if your financial institution can transfer your pre-authorized debits for you. If not, you’ll need to set them up on the new account and cancel them on the old account.
Learn more about requesting a stop payment.

### Review your accounts after the transfer
Review both accounts before closing the old one.
Make sure you:

- can access your new chequing and savings accounts
- received your new debit card, if applicable
- changed your direct deposits
- cancelled or transferred all pre-authorized debits
- requested stop payments for any cheques you wrote

### Tip
Don’t close your old accounts too soon.
You may want to keep them open for several months and make sure there’s enough money to cover payments.

### Close your accounts
Once you’re sure all transactions are complete, ask your current financial institution to close your old accounts.
You may have to pay a closing fee. Check the terms and conditions of your account agreement for more information.

## Transferring your credit card
You may cancel your credit card and get a new one with another financial institution. You must qualify for the new card. You may also be able to transfer your current credit card balance to it.
Before you cancel your current credit card, make sure you:

- qualify for the new card
- know all your automated transactions on your old card
- have a plan to pay the balance
- understand the terms and conditions of the new card

### Apply for a new credit card
Before you complete a credit card application, read the terms and conditions. When the card arrives, you’ll need to activate it before you use it.
Learn more about applying for a credit card.

### Identify your automated transactions
Review your statements to identify automated transactions tied to the current card.

### Review your credit card balance
Review your balance and decide how you’ll pay it. You may be able to transfer the balance to a new card through a balance transfer.
You usually need to pay a fee to transfer a balance from one credit card to another. The transaction may take several weeks to appear in your account.
Learn how credit cards work.

### Set up your automated transactions
Set up all the automated transactions you identified on your new card. This includes pre-authorized payments and recurring transactions.

### Monitor your credit card accounts
Before you cancel your old credit card, transfer or stop all your automated transactions. This includes any pre-authorized payments.
Some charges may still go on your credit card even after you cancel it.

### Cancel your credit card
Contact your financial institution to cancel the old credit card. Cutting up the card doesn’t cancel it.
Check both credit card accounts until all automated transactions are transferred. Make sure you’ve settled the old account.
Learn more about cancelling your credit card.

## Transferring your loan or line of credit
Some financial institutions let you transfer loans and lines of credit, while others don’t. You may need to pay off your current loan or line of credit first.
In most cases, you may need to apply for a new loan or line of credit to pay off the old one. You’ll need to qualify for a new loan or line of credit with your new financial institution.
You may have to pay fees if you pay off your loan or line of credit early. Ask your new financial institution about your payoff options and any early payment fees.

### Tip
Check if your new financial institution offers debt consolidation.
It combines your loans and lines of credit into one loan and may give you a lower interest rate.

Learn more about loans and lines of credit.
Learn more about debt consolidation.

## Transferring your mortgage
You can’t transfer your existing mortgage to another lender.
To switch lenders, you must break your current mortgage contract. You must also qualify for a new mortgage with a new lender.
You may need to pay fees, like:

- a prepayment penalty
- mortgage discharge fees
- setup fees with the new lender, which may include registration and assignment fees from your current lender
- an appraisal to confirm your property’s value
- other fees, including administration fees
Learn more about switching your mortgage to another lender.
Learn more about breaking your mortgage contract.

## Transferring your registered product
Registered products have their own transfer process. You’ll need to complete and sign transfer forms.
The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) provides information on how to transfer registered products.
These products include:

- Registered Retirement Savings Plans (RRSP)
- Tax-Free Savings Accounts (TFSA)
- Registered Retirement Income Funds (RRIF)
- Registered Disability Savings Plans (RDSP)
- Registered Education Savings Plans (RESP)
Before transferring a registered account to another financial institution, ask whether your current provider charges a transfer-out fee. Some providers reimburse these fees, but conditions may apply.
Federally regulated financial institutions must transfer Registered Plans within set timelines.
Learn more about savings and pension plans.
Learn more about transferring registered plans between banks.

## Checking your records after the transfer
After you transfer your products or services, you may ask your financial institution for a list of the accounts you closed. You may also ask for a list of the products or services you cancelled.

### Check your credit report
Check your credit report to confirm that your financial institution closed your accounts.
You may order your credit report from Canada’s two main credit bureaus:

- Equifax
- TransUnion
They usually take 30 to 90 days to update information on your credit report.
Learn more about getting your credit report and score.

### Cancel your online banking access
When you no longer have any products or services with that financial institution, cancel your online banking access.
You may cancel your access:

- by phone
- online
- at your branch
Learn more about online banking.
