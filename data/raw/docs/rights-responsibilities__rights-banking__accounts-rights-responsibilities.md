# Opening a personal bank account: your rights

Learn about opening a personal bank account to manage your money and access everyday banking services. Find out what information banks must give you and how your rights are protected.

## Understanding your rights when opening a personal bank account
You have rights when opening a personal bank account. These rights apply when you deal with a bank, authorized foreign bank or federal credit union.
This page uses the term bank to refer to federally regulated financial institutions generally.
Learn more about how your banking rights are protected.
You have the right to open a personal bank account at a bank if you meet certain conditions.
To open an account, you must provide acceptable forms of identification. Banks may accept different types of identification in certain situations.
Learn more about the identification you can use to open a bank account.
Before opening your account, a bank must:

- obtain your express consent
- enter into an agreement with you
- provide you with a copy of the account agreement
The bank’s communications with you must be made in a manner, and using language, that is clear, simple and not misleading when obtaining express consent.
Learn more about express consent for financial products and services.

### Receiving information when opening a personal bank account
Banks must provide key information in writing when you open an account. You may receive this information on paper or electronically if you consent.
The information includes:

- a copy of the account agreement before you consent to open the account in person
- a written copy of the account agreement without delay if you open an account by phone
- a list of all charges that apply to the account
- the interest rate if applicable, like how the bank calculates it and how often it pays interest
- maximum cheque hold periods
- the bank’s policies when the maximum cheque hold periods don’t apply
- the circumstances in which the bank will send you an electronic alert for your personal account
Opened an account by phone? You have 14 business days to close it, free of charge. The bank must refund any fees you paid to operate the account, except for interest charges.

## Receiving information when opening a joint bank account
Joint bank accounts are also known as joint deposit accounts.
Some banks are members of the Canadian Bankers Association (CBA). These banks have signed a commitment to share information with consumers who open joint bank accounts.
Banks make this information available in their branches and on their websites.
This applies if you:

- open a new personal account with another person or persons
- convert a personal bank account under 1 name to an account that more than 1 person holds jointly
The commitment sets out the information banks make available to consumers. Banks agree to provide the information in a language and manner that is clear, simple and not misleading.
Banks will also make the commitment available:

- at each branch in Canada where they offer products or services
- at each of their points of service
- on each of their websites through which they offer products or services in Canada
Learn more about CBA's Commitment on Powers of Attorney and Joint Deposit Accounts.
You may be dealing with a financial institution that isn’t a bank that signed a commitment to share information with consumers who open joint bank accounts. In that case, check your account agreement or ask them for details about joint bank accounts.
Learn more about joint accounts.

## Receiving electronic alerts
Your bank is required to send you an electronic alert without delay when the balance of your chequing or savings account falls below a certain amount.
These alerts may help you manage your day-to-day finances and avoid fees.
Learn more about your right to receive electronic alerts.

## When a bank may refuse to open an account
A bank may refuse to open a personal bank account for you if they have reasonable grounds to believe:

- you plan to use the account for illegal or fraudulent purposes
- you knowingly made false statements in the information you gave
- it’s necessary to protect their customers or employees from physical harm, harassment or other abuse
A bank may also refuse if:

- you’ve had a history of illegal or fraudulent activity with financial service providers in the last 7 years
- you don’t allow them to verify that the identification you presented is valid
- the branch or point of service only offers accounts linked to another financial institution and you don’t have one
If a bank refuses to open a personal bank account for you, they must inform you in writing.
The bank must also disclose to you:

- their procedure for dealing with complaints
- the contact information for the external complaints body: the Ombudsman for Banking Services and Investments (OBSI)
- the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada’s mailing address, website address and telephone number
You may file a complaint with your bank if you’re dissatisfied with how they treated you.
Learn how to file a complaint with your bank.

## Receiving notice of increased charges or new charges
Your bank may increase charges or introduce new charges. If you receive account statements, the bank must give you information about the change in writing. The bank must do this at least 30 days before the change takes effect.
You may receive the information on paper or electronically if you consent.
The bank must also disclose this information by:

- displaying it prominently
- at each of its branches in Canada offering products or services
- at each of its points of service
- on each of its websites offering products or services in Canada

- providing or sending it in writing (on request)
The bank must also display a notice prominently at their automated teller machines (ATMs) for customers who don’t receive account statements. The bank must do this at least 60 days before the change takes effect.

### Refunds and credits (redress)
Banks must refund or credit any fees or penalties they’ve charged you for a financial product or service if:

- they didn’t disclose the charges or penalties to you in an agreement, or they disclosed them incorrectly
- you didn’t provide express consent for the product or service
Banks must also pay applicable interest on these amounts.
