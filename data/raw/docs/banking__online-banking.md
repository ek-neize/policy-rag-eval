# Online banking

Learn how to use online banking to manage your accounts and complete everyday transactions online. Learn how online banking works and how to protect your personal and financial information. Understand what you’re responsible for when you bank online.

## Using online banking
Online banking lets you access your accounts online to complete financial transactions. For example, you may use online banking to pay bills or transfer money between accounts.
You may bank online using a computer or a mobile device, like a phone or tablet.
Before using online banking, you may need to:

- register for online banking with your financial institution
- enter your debit card number
- create a username and password
- read and accept the terms and conditions
Online banking transactions usually cost less than using:

- in-person teller services
- automated teller machines (ATMs)

### Tip
Stay on top of your money in real time. Online banking lets you check transactions as they happen, no need to wait for a monthly statement.

### Using online banking on a computer
To access your accounts online, you must:

- go to your financial institution’s website
- enter:
- your username or account number
- your password
- answers to your security questions

- complete the 2-step verification process if it's set up on your account
Online banking on a computer allows you to:

- manage your accounts and payments:
- access your accounts
- make future-dated payments
- transfer funds between accounts
- schedule regular payments or transfers

- apply for and set up services:
- open a new account
- apply for loans
- access your monthly statements

### Tip
Act quickly if something looks suspicious. Some financial institutions let you block access to your debit or credit cards online if you notice unusual activity.

### Using online banking on a mobile device
You may use mobile banking wherever you take your mobile device, like cell phones and tablets.
To access your accounts on a mobile device online, you may use:

- a mobile banking application (app)
- your financial institution’s online banking site in a web browser

### Tip
Know what your app can do. Some banking apps allow only certain transactions, like transfers or bill payments. To access all services, use your financial institution’s online banking site.

#### Mobile banking application (app)
To access your accounts using an app, you must:

- download your financial institution’s mobile banking app to your mobile device
- open the app on your device
- sign in using your username or account number and password

Only download banking apps and updates from trusted sources, like:

- your financial institution’s website
- your device’s app store

#### Mobile banking sites
A mobile banking site lets you access your accounts on your mobile device without downloading an app.
Using a web browser on your mobile device is like using online banking on a computer. Mobile banking sites may give you a more detailed view of your accounts. They may also offer more services than a mobile banking app.

## Protecting yourself when using online banking
To protect your information when you bank online:

- check your accounts regularly for suspicious or unauthorized transactions
- use a strong password that no one can easily guess
- use different passwords for each of your accounts
- don’t give your usernames, passwords or personal identification numbers (PINs) to anyone, not even family members
- don’t respond to suspicious email or text messages or click on links within them

### Tip
Act quickly if the device you use for online banking is lost or stolen. Contact your financial institution right away. You may also contact your mobile service provider to block your account.

Learn more about how to secure your accounts.

### Personal financial information
Protect yourself when banking online to help prevent criminals from capturing or copying your financial information.
Someone may see what you send over public networks. For example, networks you use in coffee shops, bookstores and public libraries.
This information may include:

- passwords
- bank account numbers
- credit card numbers
- personal information
To protect your financial information when banking online:

- don’t use public Wi-Fi or public computers
- if you must use a public computer, clear its history and cache when you finish using it
- don’t store your usernames and passwords on mobile devices
- don’t allow your computer or mobile device to save your password or banking information, like your account number
- don’t share your personal information by email or text message

## Understanding your responsibilities when banking online
To stay protected from financial loss from an unauthorized transaction:

- don’t give your PIN, password or security answers to anyone, not even family
- follow your user agreement for how to use and protect your banking information, like log-in details and passwords
- check your accounts regularly for transactions you didn’t make or approve
- contact your financial institution or service provider as soon as you find an error or suspicious transaction
You may need to take more steps to protect yourself when banking online. Read your account agreement. Check your financial institution’s online banking or electronic access agreement to learn more.

### Unauthorized transactions
Most financial institutions have policies that protect you from unauthorized transactions. This includes transactions you didn’t make or approve.

You're responsible for keeping your banking and online banking details to yourself.

If you give your banking information to anyone, including a spouse, partner, family member or friend, you may:

- lose the protection against unauthorized transactions offered by your financial institution
- be responsible for any unauthorized transactions
Contact your financial institution about its online banking policies and protections.
Learn more about your rights and responsibilities for unauthorized credit or debit transactions.

### Passwords and PINs
Choose strong passwords for online banking accounts. If you use a password that’s easy to guess, you may be responsible for transactions you didn’t make or approve.

### Tip
Strengthen your passwords. Create a unique password for each account. Use a mix of upper- and lower-case letters, numbers and symbols.

Malware (malicious software) can damage your devices and put your financial information at risk. Malware can expose your passwords and PINs. If that happens, you may be responsible for unauthorized transactions.
