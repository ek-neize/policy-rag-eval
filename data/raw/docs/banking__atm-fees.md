# Automated teller machine (ATM) fees

Learn more about the fees you may pay when you use an ATM. Learn how different ATM owners charge fees and how to reduce costs.

## Understanding ATM fees
An automated teller machine (ATM) fee is a charge you may pay when you withdraw cash from an ATM.
You may need to pay ATM fees in Canada. You may pay more fees if you use ATMs outside Canada.
ATM fees may vary depending on who owns the ATM:

- your financial institution
- another financial institution
- a private operator

### Types of ATM fees
You may pay one or more fees when you withdraw cash from an ATM, like:

- a withdrawal fee from your account
- a network access fee
- a convenience fee charged by the ATM owner

### Tip
Before making a withdrawal, make sure you understand which fees apply. The total cost of using an ATM that isn't operated by your financial institution can be significantly higher.

#### Regular account fee
Your financial institution may charge this fee each time you withdraw cash from an ATM.
Some service packages include a certain number of ATM withdrawals at no extra cost.

#### Network access fee
Your financial institution may charge this fee if you use an ATM they don’t own. They add this fee to your regular account fees.

#### Convenience fee
A private ATM operator or a financial institution where you don’t have an account may charge this fee.
The financial institution may add the convenience fee to the:

- network access fee
- regular account fee

## Receiving information about ATM fees
If you open a bank account at a federally regulated financial institution, they must give you information about account fees. This includes details about ATM withdrawal fees and any limits or conditions that apply.
Learn more about opening a bank account.

## Reducing ATM withdrawal fees
To help reduce or avoid ATM fees:

- consider how much money you need before you withdraw cash
- use your financial institution's ATMs when you withdraw cash
- stay within your monthly transaction limit by making fewer withdrawals
- use your debit card for purchases since merchants usually don’t charge fees
- avoid using a credit card for a cash advance at an ATM
- ask for cash back from merchants that offer it at no charge

### Tip
Cash back lets you get cash at the checkout when you pay by debit. The merchant adds the amount to your purchase total and gives it to you in cash. It’s a simple way to access cash without using an ATM.
