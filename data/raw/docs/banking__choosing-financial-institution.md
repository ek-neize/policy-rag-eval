# Choosing a financial institution

Learn about choosing a financial institution that meets your needs. Compare fees, interest rates, and access to services like branches, ATMs and online banking.

## Determining what products and services you need
Before you choose a financial institution, make a list of the products and services you need. This may help you choose the type of financial institution that best meets your needs.
Some financial institutions offer many products and services. Others focus on specific ones.
Learn how to choose financial products and services that are right for you.

## Deciding if you want all your products and services with one financial institution
You may want to keep all your products and services with one financial institution.
Benefits may include:

- access all your products and services through one website or mobile app
- easily track your income and expenses
- get offers or discounts
- get advice that fits your needs
- transfer money between accounts at no cost
Keeping all your products and services with one financial institution can also have downsides.
For some products or services, other financial institutions may offer better options, like:

- lower fees
- better interest rates
- more favourable loan terms
- better investment services
You may also:

- miss out on new or better features like budgeting tools
- face more risk if the financial institution has problems
- be more affected by a data breach since all your accounts are in one place

### Tip
The financial institution needs to approve you for products and services. This includes credit cards, loans, lines of credit and mortgages. You may not qualify for every product at one financial institution.

### Fees and interest rates
Compare fees and interest rates for what you need. Fees can vary by financial institution and may change over time.
They may include:

- monthly or annual fees
- administrative fees
- automated teller machine (ATM) fees

Use the Account Comparison Tool to compare bank account interest rates, fees and services

Use the Credit Card Comparison Tool to compare interest rates, fees, rewards and more

Financial institutions also set the interest rates for their products.
This includes products where you pay or earn interest, like:

- mortgages
- loans
- lines of credit
- savings accounts
- some investments
The Bank of Canada’s policy interest rate typically affects the interest rates that financial institutions set.
Learn more about the policy interest rate on the Bank of Canada's website.

### Convenience and access
Think about how you want to access your money and services. If branch and ATM access matters to you, look for locations that are easy to reach.
In some areas, there may be only a few branches. Larger financial institutions often have more branches and ATMs.
If you use online banking, compare online and mobile services. Check ease of use, security features and app ratings.

### Tip
Planning to travel or live abroad? Check if your financial institution offers ATM access and other services outside Canada.

### Customer service
Financial institutions generally set their own customer service rules.
To learn about customer service, you can:

- read customer reviews (for example, on review sites or forums)
- ask friends and family for recommendations
- visit a local branch to see if staff are helpful and responsive

### Direct deposit and cheque cashing
Some financial institutions may let you set up direct deposit for government benefits and tax refunds. This may help if you need your money right away or can’t get to a branch.
Some financial institutions may also give you access to the first $100 when you cash a cheque.
Learn more about cashing a cheque.

### Online services
Larger financial institutions may spend more on technology. They may offer better online banking, mobile apps and other online tools.

## Understanding types of financial institutions and companies
There are many types of financial institutions in Canada. Some companies also offer financial products and services, like investments and payday loans.

### Tip
Not all financial institutions and other companies offer the same products and services. Compare your options and find what fits your needs.

### Banks
Banks offer many products and services.
For example, they offer:

- chequing accounts
- savings accounts
- loans
- mortgages
- investment products

### Cooperatives
A cooperative is a business that is organized and run on a cooperative basis. There are different types of cooperatives, like credit unions.

Credit unions and Desjardins credit unions (caisses populaires Desjardins) A credit union is a financial cooperative owned by its members. It offers different products like savings and chequing accounts, loans, mortgages and investments. Unlike a bank, members take part in the governance of the institution.
Desjardins credit unions are part of the Desjardins Group, a cooperative financial group. Like other credit unions, they offer different products and services to their members.

Other cooperatives The financial sector also includes other cooperatives, like cooperative insurance companies. These cooperatives also operate based on a cooperative ownership model.

### Loan companies
Loan companies focus on loans.
For example, they offer:

- personal loans
- car loans
- small business loans
- payday loans
Learn more about loans.

### Trust companies
Trust companies offer services related to estate planning and investments. They also offer fiduciary services.
For example, they offer:

- trustee or executor services
- powers of attorney
- investment management
- employee benefit plans

### Mortgage companies
Mortgage companies focus on mortgages and mortgage-related services. Examples include mortgage brokers and mortgage investment corporations (MIC).

Mortgage brokers Mortgage brokers help you find a mortgage lender. They also help you apply for a mortgage. Unlike most other mortgage companies, they work with all types of borrowers.

Mortgage investment corporations Mortgage investment corporations buy and sell mortgage loans. They often work with people who may not qualify for a mortgage with a financial institution.
They invest in mortgages or mortgage-backed securities. They raise money from investors to fund mortgages. Investors earn money from the interest borrowers pay.
Learn more about mortgages.

### Insurance companies
Insurance companies focus on insurance products, including:

- home
- car
- life
- health
They may also offer investment and savings products.
Learn more about insurance.

### Securities firms
Securities firms help buy and sell securities. They offer investment advice and manage investments.
Examples include:

- brokerage firms
- mutual fund dealers
- investment dealers
Learn more about investments on your securities regulator’s website.

## Checking what protections apply to you
Before you choose a financial institution, check what protections apply. These are your rights when you deal with a financial institution or company.
This may affect:

- what information you get about a product or service
- what information you get when you apply for and get a product or service
- how and when you have the right to get information
- how you can make a complaint and how it’s resolved
- how deposit insurance protects your deposits
- your protection against transactions you didn’t approve
The level of protection depends on the regulator.
Learn more about federal oversight bodies and other regulators.

### Federally regulated financial institutions
The federal government regulates some financial institutions. They may operate anywhere in Canada. They’re often larger and may offer more products and services.
Examples include:

- all banks
- federal credit unions
- federal trust and loan companies
- insurance companies
Federal regulators make sure these financial institutions follow federal laws and rules. This helps keep them financially sound and helps them meet required standards.

Laws and rules help keep Canada’s financial system safe.
They also help protect your rights when you deal with a financial institution.

Check if your financial institution is federally regulated.
Learn more about your financial rights and responsibilities.

### Provincial and territorial financial institutions and companies
Provinces and territories regulate financial institutions and companies that are not federally regulated. They usually operate only in that province or territory.
Many offer products and services like federally regulated financial institutions. Each province and territory has its own regulators.
They oversee these financial institutions, companies and most investment products and services. The laws and rules may vary from one province or territory to another.
Learn more about provincial and territorial regulators.

### Deposit insurance
If your financial institution fails, deposit insurance protects eligible deposits up to a set limit.
The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protects eligible deposits at federally regulated financial institutions that are members. The limit is $100,000 for each type of deposit at each member institution.
If your financial institution isn’t federally regulated, your province or territory may protect your deposits. Coverage and limits vary across Canada.

### Tip
Check what’s protected by contacting your financial institution or deposit insurer.
