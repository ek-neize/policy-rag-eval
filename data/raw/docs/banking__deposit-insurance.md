# Deposit insurance in Canada

Learn about deposit insurance and how it protects your deposits if your financial institution fails. Learn what the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) covers at member institutions. Learn what your province covers for provincially regulated financial institutions.

## Understanding deposit insurance
Deposit insurance protects your deposits if your financial institution fails.
The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) covers eligible deposits at member institutions. You don’t need to apply or pay for this.

You don’t need to file a claim if your institution fails. CDIC pays you automatically.

Check if your financial institution is a member of the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation website.

## Checking what the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation covers
CDIC covers eligible deposits separately for up to $100,000, including:

- savings and chequing accounts
- Guaranteed Investment Certificates (GICs) and other term deposits
- foreign currency, like US dollars
CDIC doesn’t cover:

- mutual funds
- stocks
- bonds
- Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)
- cryptocurrencies
- losses due to fraud or theft

## Contacting your province’s deposit insurer
Provincial deposit insurance plans cover deposits in:

- credit unions
- trust and loan companies
- caisses populaires
Deposit insurance plans vary by province.
Contact your deposit insurer or your financial institution to learn how they protect your deposits:

- Alberta
- British Columbia
- Manitoba
- New Brunswick
- Newfoundland and Labrador
- Nova Scotia
- Ontario
- Prince Edward Island
- Quebec
- Saskatchewan
